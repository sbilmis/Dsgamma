## Intercept-ratio choice and separate Borel ratio

The two agreed domain parameters do not fix both intercepts. Define

    eta = tilde_s_A / tilde_s_P,
    tilde_s_P = s_eff*(1+eta)/eta,
    tilde_s_A = s_eff*(1+eta).

Following the geometric explanation and subsequent plan agreement, the
central prescription is `eta=m_A^2/m_P^2` and separately
`r=M_A^2/M_P^2=m_A^2/m_P^2`. These are specified modelling/auxiliary choices,
not exact QCD identities. Keep the explicit fresh-session execution hold
at the top of this README.

Keep the intercept ratio fixed when scanning the auxiliary Borel ratio;
threshold-domain and Borel-ratio sensitivity must remain distinguishable.

The explanation and geometric illustration are in `discussion/`. With a=1,
the boundary is s_A=(1+eta)*s_eff-eta*s_P: a=1 fixes linearity, s_eff fixes
the point (s_eff,s_eff), and eta fixes its slope. For Ds2536 at s_eff=9.5,
equal intercepts give (tilde_s_P,tilde_s_A)=(19,19) GeV^2; the mass-ratio
assignment eta=1.658793763 gives (15.227053,25.258541) GeV^2. These are
geometrical intercepts, not the separate two-point continuum thresholds.
The figure evaluates no OPE or widths.

The two ratios eta (domain) and r (Borel) are independent modelling/auxiliary
quantities. In fact the cited paper's Table 2 chooses mass-based intercept
ratios while Eq. (3.12) chooses equal Borel parameters in its numerical run.
A plain delta(s_P-s_A) density only sees s_eff; delta derivatives make the
boundary slope relevant. The equal-intercept assignment remains a proposed
sensitivity control, with all other inputs held identical. No such numerical
comparison has yet run.
