\noindent\textbf{Proposed response paragraph:}

To examine the sensitivity to the condensate input, we performed a paired
numerical comparison using a reconstruction of the earlier sum-rule
expressions. We changed the central value at $\mu=1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ from
\begin{equation}
\langle\bar q q\rangle_{\mathrm{old}}
=-0.0165\,\mathrm{GeV}^3
\quad\text{to}\quad
\langle\bar q q\rangle_{\mathrm{new}}
=-(0.240)^3\,\mathrm{GeV}^3
=-0.013824\,\mathrm{GeV}^3,
\end{equation}
holding all other inputs and the sum-rule prescription fixed.
The strange and mixed condensates were changed consistently through
\begin{equation}
\langle\bar s s\rangle=0.8\langle\bar q q\rangle,
\qquad
\langle\bar s g_s\sigma\!\cdot\!G s\rangle
=m_0^2\langle\bar s s\rangle,
\qquad m_0^2=0.8\,\mathrm{GeV}^2.
\end{equation}
At the working-window midpoints, $(M^2,s_0)=(4.5,10)\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$
for $D_s$ and $(11,43)\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ for $B_s$, the shifts
$\Delta\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{new}}-\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$ are
\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{D_s}=+0.185^\circ,
\qquad
\Delta\theta_{B_s}=+0.057^\circ.
\end{equation}
A $21\times21$ grid over each earlier working region gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{D_s}=0.119^\circ\text{--}0.368^\circ,
\qquad
\Delta\theta_{B_s}=0.044^\circ\text{--}0.080^\circ,
\end{equation}
for $M^2=3$--$6\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$, $s_0=9$--$11\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$
in the charm-strange sector and $M^2=9$--$13\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$,
$s_0=42$--$44\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ in the bottom-strange sector.
Thus, within this comparison, the condensate-induced shifts remain smaller
than the quoted angle uncertainties of $0.6^\circ$ and $0.1^\circ$ throughout
the sampled regions. This is a central-input sensitivity check; it is not
a new extraction of the mixing angles or a propagation of all input
uncertainties. We retain the published mixing angles as external inputs.
